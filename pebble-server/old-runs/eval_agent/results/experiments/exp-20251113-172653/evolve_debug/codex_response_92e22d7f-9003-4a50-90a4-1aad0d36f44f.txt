**Behavior & Performance – Confidence: Medium**  
- P99 latency was stable only up to 60 k req/s (6.56 ms at 50 k, 17.7 ms at 60 k) but collapsed into multi‑second stalls beyond that: 2.56 s at 80 k, 4.42 s at 100 k and 4.02 s at 140 k (results/experiments/exp-20251113-172653/summary.json:30-120). The previous revision held 11.7 ms at 80 k and 0.75 s at 100 k (parent_metrics), so most headroom vanished.  
- Server logs show the new selector drove traffic into only 4–5 of the 6 workers for every rate—e.g., only workers 0/1/4/5 at 50 k (results/run-20251113-172653/logs/server/server.log:4-58), only 1/2/4/5 at 80 k (results/run-20251113-172733/logs/server/server.log:4-63), only 1/3/4/5 at 100 k (results/run-20251113-172753/logs/server/server.log:4-58) and only 0/1/2/5 at 140 k (results/run-20251113-172813/logs/server/server.log:4-38). Two workers sit idle each time, so the effective fan‑out is 33 % smaller and each active worker sees a higher proportion of 2 ms scans, amplifying head‑of‑line blocking.  
- Because the simplifying change now makes exactly one `hash % active` choice per packet (ebpf/agent.c:57-74) whereas the prior program iterated over up to `active` slots until one succeeded (ebpf/agent_backup.c:29-52), any hash collisions from the seven UDP client sockets strand the unreferenced workers. The logs confirm this skew while the average per-worker GET latency stays near 10 µs, so the database layer was still fast—the bottleneck is purely at the reuseport/BPF routing layer.  
- Clients accumulate huge outstanding counts and miss responses once the skewed workers’ receive queues overflow: at 80 k req/s 22 k responses never arrive (results/run-20251113-172733/logs/client.log:2-18); at 100 k, 109 k are missing (results/run-20251113-172753/logs/client.log:2-18); at 140 k, 245 k go missing (results/run-20251113-172813/logs/client.log:2-18). The lost traffic plus queued packets explain the multi‑second tail latencies.  

**Did it achieve the program’s intent?**  
Intent (from ebpf/agent.c:1-21) was to preserve the modulo policy from the prior best run while shrinking instruction count to improve p99. That intent failed: the deterministic single-shot mapping exercised only a subset of workers, so throughput plateaued around 70–80 k req/s and p99 ballooned into multi‑second waits, far worse than the parent that evenly fanned out traffic. Evidence: skewed worker stats across all runs (server logs above), sustained packet loss reported by the clients, and p99 regressions in results/experiments/exp-20251113-172653/summary.json:70-120 compared to the parent metrics.

- **What:** Instrument the server to export per-worker reuseport selection counts vs. “expected” counts (e.g., evenly split) and flag when any worker stays at zero. **How:** extend `startStatsLogger` in internal/server/server.go to include a simple histogram of packets per worker each second. **Why:** would immediately reveal selector skew without combing through logs, catching issues like today’s stranded workers before running full sweeps.  
- **What:** Track `bpf_sk_select_reuseport` helper return codes or slot IDs inside the eBPF program and expose them via a perf event or map sampled by userspace. **How:** add a BPF per-slot counter map incremented before/after the helper call. **Why:** would confirm whether certain hash values never map to some slots or whether the helper refuses particular sockets, resolving the remaining uncertainty about why workers disappear.
