**Behavior & Performance**  
- 50–80 k req/s loads stayed stable: throughput matched the offered rate (49–79 k responses/s) and p99 latencies were 5.9/3.7/9.8 ms for 50/60/80 k respectively, showing the hybrid selector cuts tail latency compared with the prior 18.8 ms at 60 k because it avoids the old 128-iteration retry loop and lets half the traffic ride the kernel’s backlog-aware path (`results/run-20251113-170344/workload_summary.json:11`, `results/run-20251113-170404/workload_summary.json:11`, `results/run-20251113-170424/workload_summary.json:11`).  
- Despite launching six workers, only a subset ever sees packets: at 50 k the server log prints traffic for workers 1–4 while IDs 0 and 5 never appear, proving two sockets sit idle from the start (`results/run-20251113-170344/logs/server/server.log:4`, `results/run-20251113-170344/logs/server/server.log:7`). The imbalance worsens at higher load where only workers 0–2 carry the entire 140 k run (`results/run-20251113-170503/logs/server/server.log:4`, `results/run-20251113-170503/logs/server/server.log:6`).  
- At 100 k, overall and GET/SCAN p99s jump to ~4.5 s because the client builds up 20 k→187 k outstanding requests and eventually times out 112 k responses while the server still only serves four sockets (workers 1,2,4,5), so the effective service rate tops out near 80 k req/s (`results/run-20251113-170444/workload_summary.json:11`, `results/run-20251113-170444/logs/client.log:2`, `results/run-20251113-170444/logs/client.log:11`, `results/run-20251113-170444/logs/client.log:17`, `results/run-20251113-170444/logs/server/server.log:4`, `results/run-20251113-170444/logs/server/server.log:7`).  
- 140 k behaves even worse: p99 7.8 s, >550 k requests pile up, 559 k never return, and only three sockets process traffic, capping throughput at ~83 k despite 140 k offered load (`results/run-20251113-170503/workload_summary.json:11`, `results/run-20251113-170503/logs/client.log:2`, `results/run-20251113-170503/logs/client.log:11`, `results/run-20251113-170503/logs/client.log:17`, `results/run-20251113-170503/logs/server/server.log:4`).  
- With half the packets pinned to a single hashed slot and no retry when that slot’s backlog grows, the idle sockets observed above effectively shrink the worker pool (three sockets × ~27 k req/s per socket ≈ 80 k), so any offered load beyond ~80 k floods the queue while GET and SCAN latencies converge at multi-second values.  
- **Confidence: Medium** – queue-depth/`SO_RXQ_OVFL` counters would confirm the backlog hypothesis, but the consistent idle-worker evidence makes it likely.

**Did the behavior achieve the program’s intent? Why or why not?**  
- Intent: blend deterministic hashing with the kernel’s backlog-aware chooser so we get even distribution most of the time while still letting the kernel deflect bursts. At ≤80 k this mostly works—latencies remain low and throughput equals the send rate, so the policy meets its fairness/locality goal in that region (`results/run-20251113-170404/workload_summary.json:11`, `results/run-20251113-170424/workload_summary.json:11`).  
- Beyond ~80 k the behavior no longer matches the intent: the hashed half keeps feeding the same three or four sockets even as their queues explode, so the kernel never gets a chance to rebalance and both workload types experience multi-second queues plus massive drops (`results/run-20251113-170444/workload_summary.json:11`, `results/run-20251113-170444/logs/client.log:17`, `results/run-20251113-170503/workload_summary.json:11`, `results/run-20251113-170503/logs/client.log:17`). Thus the policy fails to maintain fairness or tail control when stressed.

**Missing Signals & New Metrics/Logs To Add**  
- **Per-socket backlog / drop counters** – export `SO_RXQ_OVFL` + queue depth for each worker every second (extend the existing stats logger) so we can prove whether idle sockets truly have empty queues and whether the busy ones overflow; would make it clear whether the router or userspace pacing is the choke point.  
- **BPF decision tracing** – add a tiny percpu array counting how many packets take the hashed path, how often `bpf_sk_select_reuseport` fails, and how often the program falls back to SK_PASS. Dumping those counters alongside workload metrics would confirm whether idle sockets are due to map population or selector failures and guide policy tweaks.
